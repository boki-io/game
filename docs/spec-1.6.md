# Spec v1.6: The overworld (a persistent open world with checkpoints, markers, caves and dungeons)

Builds on the hub (spec 0.4), the stages and maps (specs 0.5 and 0.6: `StageData`, `MapTheme`, `MapBuilder`, the timed arena in `main.tscn`), run progress (`RunProgress`), relics (spec 1.0), the inventory and items (specs 1.4 and 1.5) and the world design in `/Users/bok/Documents/game-characters/map/map-instructions.md` ("the guide"). `main` at `c95a188` (PR #18, the inventory screen art, is open and independent). Status: built (steps 1 to 7; build notes in section 17); rev 2 (open questions O13 to O15 added after the review; applies an independent review: `is_last_stage` and the dungeon branches of `GameSession`, a granted relic leaking into the journey, the nodes abilities need, the progress shape and key types, `last_character`, quitting a dungeon, the zsh cleanup, mask loading in exported builds, per-chunk tile layers, the depth cap, a population cap, non-unique marker names, the hub door test, a walk test with the real number of points, and cross-references).

Terms: the **world** is the 2000 x 2000 tile map `map-v3` (1 map pixel = 1 tile = 16 px, so 32,000 x 32,000 px). The **route** is the S-shaped main path (6,723 tiles) from the start camp to the final vault. A **zone** is one of the eight biomes along it. A **marker** is a place on the world listed in `map_data_v3.json` (camp, gate, checkpoint, cave, dungeon and so on). A **journey** is one character's saved game in the world. A **dungeon run** is a cave or dungeon played as arena stages. The **hub** is today's hub scene.

## Flow

In the hub you choose a character and walk through the door, as today. The door now loads the **overworld** with that character's journey: a new one at the start camp, or where you left off. You walk a huge world drawn from the map: a road and side trails through forest, swamp, mushroom thicket, graveyard, sulfur flats, volcano, snow and crystal, each biome blending into the next, with solid-looking walls where you cannot go. Markers dot the world: camps, shrines, hermit huts, the seven gates (open for now), checkpoints and the entrances of caves and dungeons. Press F at a checkpoint to rest: the game saves and you will respawn there. Press F at a cave or dungeon to enter it: the screen fades and you play it as the arena stages you know (a cave is one 5 minute stage, a boss dungeon is three stages in a row); clearing it is saved, and you come back out at its entrance. A minimap shows your surroundings and M opens the map of the parts you have discovered. Your level, upgrades and relics are saved with the journey. Enemies roaming the overworld, fights at camps, bosses and gate locks come in later specs.

## 1. Scope

In scope:
- The world data: the collision, zone and map images and the marker JSON from the guide, committed, plus a derived edge-depth map and a loader (section 3).
- The terrain: chunk streaming around the player, per-zone ground and blocker art from the eight tilesets, visible walls, collision built from the mask (section 4).
- The overworld scene: the player, camera, HUD, pause menu, the hub door, the journey and its per-character save (sections 5 and 6).
- Markers: placement of all 108, interaction, checkpoints and respawn, gates with an open/locked state (section 7).
- Caves and dungeons played as arena stages, clearing, death and abandon rules (section 8).
- Fog of war, a minimap and a view-only world map (section 9).
- Tests and docs (sections 12 and 13).

Out of scope (decided with the user): enemies in the overworld (patrols, camps, arenas, difficulty by route progress); bosses and any lock on a gate (gates open, with a hook); rewards at shrines, ruins, altars and huts (they are placed and named, with no effect); caches' loot; fast travel (checkpoints save and respawn only); real structure art (placeholders drawn in code); per-zone ambience and music; locking the world's unreachable parts differently from the mask; changing the old stage select (it stays in the code, no door reaches it).

Out of scope (later specs, as the guide's order suggests): overworld enemies and camps; the nine bosses; gate locks; special-site rewards; fast travel; real structure art; zone sound.

## 2. Decisions taken (from the user's answers)

- **Scope:** world foundation and dungeon entry, with a minimap and fog.
- **Progress is persistent:** a journey saves level, XP, level-up upgrades, relics, position, activated checkpoints, cleared markers and the discovered map. Death never resets any of it.
- **The hub stays:** its door leads to the overworld, resuming that character's journey; the chest, camp shop and character change stay in the hub.
- **One journey per character:** each of the three characters has their own journey file. Gold, camp attributes and the inventory (items) stay shared, as today.
- **Terrain uses the eight tilesets** by zone (zone id equals the dungeon map id), blended between zones.
- **Gates are open now and lockable later.**
- **A cave is one stage and a dungeon three stages;** the layout seed is fixed per marker; clearing is saved per marker; cleared ones can be re-entered.
- **Death inside a cave or dungeon returns the player to its entrance;** an overworld death (when enemies exist) respawns at the last checkpoint. Everything is kept.
- **Checkpoints save and respawn only.**

## 3. The world data and `WorldData`

The zone ids are the ids of `content/maps/<id>` (verified: `mossy_hollow`, `flooded_gallery`, `spore_cavern`, `bone_warren`, `sulfur_pits`, `ember_depths`, `frostbite_grotto`, `crystal_vault`).

- **Files committed** under `content/overworld/data/`: `collision.png`, `zones.png`, `world_map.png` (the art, 2000 x 2000), `markers.json` (the guide's `map_data_v3.json`), and a derived `edge_depth.png`. `art/source/overworld/` keeps `map-instructions.md`, `generate_map.py` and `render_map.py` for provenance (the guide says to re-run the generator rather than hand-edit). The 2.3 MB `.aseprite` files stay outside the repo. Image imports use lossless, no mipmaps, nearest filtering (linear only for the world map's downscaled draw).
- **`tools/overworld/build_edge_depth.py`** (pure Python, no Pillow, as `build_demon_head.py` is) writes `edge_depth.png`: for every blocked tile the distance in tiles (Chebyshev) to the nearest walkable tile, capped at 15; 0 for walkable tiles. `--check` compares. This is the only preprocessing: it lets the terrain find the visible edge ring and the collider ring without searching at run time.
- **`WorldData`** (`scripts/overworld/world_data.gd`, `class_name WorldData`, a `RefCounted`): loads the three images into `PackedByteArray`s (4 MB each) and the JSON once, through `load()` of the imported texture, `get_image()` and `convert(Image.FORMAT_L8)` (an exported build has no `res://*.png` bytes to read, only the imported copies; `Image.load_from_file` would fail there), asserting `get_data().size() == 4_000_000`, then drops the textures so the masks cost no video memory; the three mask imports use `compress/mode` lossless (0), no mipmaps; `is_walkable(tile)`, `zone_at(tile)`, `depth_at(tile)`, `tile_of(world_pos)`, `world_of(tile)` (`tile * 16 + 8`), `markers` (an `Array[Dictionary]`), `marker_named(name)` (only for the kinds whose names are unique: `start_camp`, `checkpoint`, `cave`, `dungeon`, `final_dungeon`; four special markers share names, so specials, camps and caches are always addressed by their index in the JSON, `marker_at(index)`), `zones`, and `start_marker()`. Out-of-range tiles are blocked, zone 0, depth 15. Loaded lazily on the first use and cached in a static.
- **Integrity** is a test (section 12), not a runtime check: every marker stands on a walkable tile; every marker is reachable from the start camp over walkable tiles; the main path is walkable; the zone value of every walkable tile is 0 to 7.

## 4. The terrain (`WorldTerrain`)

Verified: the arena uses a `TileMapLayer` (`Ground`, `TILE = 16`) and static bodies on collision layer 1 for walls; the player is a `CharacterBody2D` with layer 2 and mask 1 (so it collides with layer 1); `CameraFollow` limits itself to `Arena.world_size` and `Arena` builds four wall bodies and a world size from its `size` export (a `Vector2i`).

- **Chunks:** 32 x 32 tiles (512 px; 63 x 63 chunks, the last row and column partial). The terrain keeps the chunks within 1 of the player's chunk loaded (3 x 3) and frees chunks more than 2 away; at most one chunk is built per physics frame, nearest first, so no frame builds more than about 1,100 cells.
- **Drawing:** each loaded chunk is its own node: a `Node2D` holding two `TileMapLayer`s (a `ground` layer for the ground and wall tiles, an `overlay` layer for the lit edge and the shadow, so one cell can hold both) and its collision body; unloading a chunk is a `queue_free` (at most 2 chunks freed per frame), so no frame erases thousands of cells. For each tile of a chunk: walkable gives a ground tile of its zone, chosen by a stable hash of the tile so it never flickers; blocked with `edge_depth` 1 to 15 gives that zone's blocker tile (the darker `blocker_deep` for depth above 3, so the wall looks deep); blocked tiles of depth above 15 are left empty (the depth cap is 15, so a blocked tile is drawn wherever the cap is not reached; the measured view is 20 tiles each way and a 13-tile trail plus 15 tiles of wall is enough that no clear colour shows on screen), and the camera clear colour is a near-black. A blocked tile's visible edge toward a walkable tile gets the zone's edge tile in the overlay (the lit lip), and the lower-right side of every blocker the shadow tile (the art assumes light from the top left). A tile's zone is the pixel value of `zones.png`, so biomes blend exactly as the map does; no hard rectangles.
- **Tileset catalog:** `content/overworld/terrain/<zone id>.tres` (a `ZoneTerrain` resource, `scripts/overworld/zone_terrain.gd`) names, for each of the eight tileset sheets `assets/tilesets/<id>.png` (256 x 256, 16 x 16 tiles; committed in this spec, they are untracked today), the atlas coordinates of `ground` (a list of variants), `blocker`, `blocker_deep`, `edge` and `shadow`, and a fallback colour for each. The sheets are floor-tile sheets (a block of pale floor tiles over a large dark texture), not autotile sets, so the first build step looks at all eight and writes the catalog by hand; a zone whose sheet has no usable tile for a role uses the flat fallback colour for it (a generated one-colour tile), and the build notes list every such gap. The same catalog lists, per zone, the colours of its blend pair for the dither band.
- **Paths:** the guide's `paths` layer (roads and trails) is not a separate input here: the walkable corridors are the paths. A road look (a lighter ground tile along the `main_path` polyline, 6 to 7 tiles wide) is a build-time option listed in the open questions (O4).
- **Collision:** per chunk one `StaticBody2D` on layer 1 whose shapes are built from the mask: blocked tiles with `edge_depth` 1 or 2 (a 32 px ring, deeper than a physics step of the player's 90 px/s, and the player's body radius), merged into rectangles by runs along each row, then runs of equal width stacked. Walkable tiles never have a collider; interior blocked tiles (depth above 2) never do either (unreachable). The world edge uses `Arena`'s four walls. Verified count: about 15% of the 4 million tiles are walkable.
- **`Arena`:** the overworld scene has an `Arena` of size 32000 x 32000 (`wall_band` 0), so `Arena.world_size`, the camera limits and the pickups' bounds stay right; its yellow debug border (drawn at the world edge) is hidden by setting the node's `visible = false`, which hides `_draw` and keeps the wall bodies; `Arena` itself gets no change.
- **No obstacle map:** the overworld has no enemies yet, so `ObstacleMap` and `FlowField` (the arena's enemy navigation) are not built here; the later enemy spec decides how enemies navigate a chunked world.

## 5. The overworld scene

`scenes/overworld.tscn` (root script `scripts/overworld/overworld.gd`, `class_name Overworld`), built like the hub: `Arena` (first child), `Camera` (`CameraFollow`), `World` (y-sorted: `Terrain` (the `WorldTerrain`), `Markers`, `Players`, and the three nodes abilities need, as `hub.tscn` has them: `Effects` (group `effects`), `Zones` and `PlayerProjectiles` (group `player_projectiles`), which are cleared whenever the Player is rebuilt or removed, as `Hub._remove_player` does), `UI` (`Hud`, `PauseMenu`, the minimap, the world map, a prompt label, a banner label). The Player is created as the hub does (`PLAYER_SCENE`, `character`, `attribute_levels`, `equipment` from `GameSession.inventory`), with `hub_mode = false` (so mana is limited and relics and legendary grants work), at the journey's position, with the journey's progress restored by `RunProgress.restore` (level, XP, upgrades, relics) before `leveled_up` is connected, exactly as `main.gd` restores between stages.

- **HUD:** `Hud.bind(player)` with no run manager draws the mana bar and the ability icons (as the hub does); the hit points, XP bar and relic column appear when enemies do (a later spec). No timer.
- **Pause:** `PauseMenu` gets an `overworld_mode`: Continue, Return to Hub (saves the journey, then `GameSession.go_to_hub()`, no "Abandon this run?" prompt) and Exit (saves, then quits).
- **Input:** a new action `world_map` (key M, joypad Back) in `input_setup.gd`; `interact` (F) acts on markers.
- **Entering:** `GameSession.enter_overworld(character)` sets `character` and `last_character` (so the hub, on return, starts in walk mode below the door as after a run), loads the character's journey and changes to `OVERWORLD`. `Hub._enter_run` (the door) calls it instead of `start_run`. `GameSession.OVERWORLD := "res://scenes/overworld.tscn"`.
- **Leaving:** Return to Hub, and the dungeon runs (section 8), save the journey first.
- The camera follows the player with the existing dead zone. The viewport is 640 x 360 (40 x 22.5 tiles), matching the guide's assumption.

## 6. The journey and its save

`Journey` (`scripts/overworld/journey.gd`, `class_name Journey`, a `RefCounted`) holds: `character_id`, `position: Vector2` (world px), `respawn: StringName` (the name of the last activated checkpoint, or the start camp), `checkpoints: Array[StringName]` (activated, in order), `cleared: Array[StringName]` (names of cleared caves, dungeons and the final vault: unique, tested), `fog: PackedByteArray` (section 9), `progress: Dictionary` (a `RunProgress` snapshot: `level`, `xp`, `upgrades`, `relics`; never null: a new journey and a failed load give `{level: 1, xp: 0, upgrades: {}, relics: {}}`, because `RunProgress.restore` reads those keys directly; the JSON loader converts the `upgrades` and `relics` keys back to `StringName`, since `restore` looks ids up as `StringName`) and `playtime`, plus `markers_version` (a hash of `markers.json`; when it differs from the shipped file the cleared and checkpoint names are revalidated against it). `Journey.new_for(character_id, world)` starts at the start camp (its position, checkpoint activated).

- **`JourneySave`** (`scripts/overworld/journey_save.gd`, static): one file per character, `<SaveGame.path()>.journey.<character id>` (so `user://camp.json.journey.knight`); `""` when saving is off, so tests and headless runs never touch the player's files. JSON `{version: 1, position: [x, y], respawn, checkpoints, cleared, fog (base64), progress, playtime}`, written through a temp file renamed over it, loaded leniently like `InventorySave`: a missing or empty file is a new journey; invalid JSON or a wrong version is renamed `.bad` and gives a new journey; unknown marker names are dropped (the world file may change), a position on a blocked tile snaps to the respawn checkpoint, a fog of the wrong length is reset, numbers are clamped, upgrade and relic ids the registries do not know are dropped. Nothing in a journey file touches gold, camp attributes or the inventory. A journey keeps no **granted relic**: `Player._ready` grants a worn legendary item's relic and `RunProgress.capture` would store it as a permanent relic, which would survive unequipping the item in the hub. So the journey's snapshot is made by `RunProgress.capture(player, false)` (a new `include_granted` parameter, default true, so runs between stages are unchanged): it skips every id in `relics.granted`; a granted relic raised by level-up cards inside a dungeon is therefore back at level 1 in the overworld (and re-granted only while the item is worn). The fog is about 83 KB of base64, written with every save.
- **When it saves:** on activating a checkpoint, on entering a dungeon, on clearing a stage of one, on leaving it, on Return to Hub, on Exit, and every 30 s of play in the overworld while the position has moved at least 100 px (the file is written whole each time: about 90 KB).
- **`GameSession`** holds `var journey: Journey` (null outside the overworld and dungeon runs) and `enter_overworld`, `save_journey()`.
- **Per character:** choosing another pedestal and walking through the door loads that character's own file. The hub does not show journey details (a later nicety).

## 7. Markers

`OverworldMarker` (`scripts/overworld/overworld_marker.gd`, `class_name OverworldMarker`, a `Node2D`) is one marker: it keeps its data dictionary, stands at `world_x, world_y`, draws a code-drawn placeholder by `kind` (one tent-and-fire for `start_camp` and `camp`, a lamp post for `checkpoint`, two pillars and a banner across the gap for `gate`, a rock arch for `cave`, a larger arch with a skull for `dungeon` and `final_dungeon`, small shrine, ruin, altar and hut shapes for `special` by `subtype`, a mound for `cache`; `arena` markers draw nothing), and shows its name in a small label when the player is within 120 px. All 108 are created at load into `World/Markers` (cheap: each is one node with one draw), only those within 480 px of the player redraw. Placeholders are drawn centred on the marker coordinate, as the guide asks; real art comes later.

- **Interaction** is the hub's pattern: the overworld finds the nearest interactive marker within its radius (48 px; gates, camps, caches, arenas and specials are not interactive except specials, below), shows the prompt "F: Rest here" (checkpoint and start camp), "F: Enter <name>" (cave, dungeon, final vault; " (cleared)" appended when cleared) and, for specials, "F: Examine" (it shows the name and `subtype` in the banner: no reward in this spec). F has a 0.2 s lockout after a scene load or a banner, as in the hub.
- **Checkpoints and the start camp:** F activates it (once): the journey's `respawn` becomes it, it joins `checkpoints`, the journey saves, the player is healed and a banner reads "Checkpoint reached" with `Audio.play(&"stage_clear")` and a light flash (`SceneFader.flash`). An activated checkpoint draws lit. Respawn is used by later specs and by the "snap to respawn" of a bad save; there is no way to die in the overworld yet.
- **Gates:** seven markers, each drawn across its 12-tile gap along the marker's normal (`nx`, `ny`); `OverworldRules.gate_locked(name) -> bool` returns false for all today, and a locked gate would draw its pillars joined by a barrier and add a collider across the gap (the collider and art for the locked state are built and tested with a test-only flag; the shipped value is always open). The lock hook is `Journey.gate_open(name)` reading the flag the later boss spec sets.
- **Camps, caches, arenas:** placed (camps and caches drawn, arenas invisible) and inert; their data (`radius` for an arena) is kept for the enemy spec.
- **Safe radius:** no rule needed yet (no enemies); the 14 tile safe radius of the guide is a constant `OverworldRules.SAFE_RADIUS_TILES` the enemy spec will read.

## 8. Caves and dungeons as arena stages

Verified: `GameSession` holds a run's `path`, `current_stage`, `run_progress` and changes scene through `_go` (with a `scene_hook` for tests); `main.gd` builds the arena from `GameSession.current_stage.theme` and `GameSession.map_seed()`, restores `run_progress` into the new Player, and on a win calls `complete_stage()` (which captures progress and loads the select screen) or, on the last stage, `finish_run()` and the victory screen; on death `EndScreen` (DEATH) offers Return to Hub (`fail_run()`); the pause menu's Return to Hub asks "Abandon this run?" (`abandon_run()`); a stage lasts `RunManager.RUN_DURATION` = 300 s.

- **`GameSession` in a dungeon run:** `path` is null, so every place that reads it changes. `is_last_stage()` returns `dungeon.index >= dungeon.stages.size() - 1` when `dungeon != null` (the old rule is `path == null or ...`, which would make every dungeon stage the last, end the run after stage 1 and make `finish_run` read `path.column_count()` of null); `complete_stage`, `finish_run`, `fail_run` and `abandon_run` each branch on `dungeon != null` first and run the dungeon versions below; `Hud.bind` shows no stage text in a dungeon run (it needs `path`), so the HUD gets the stage text `"<marker name> - stage n of N"` from the `DungeonRun`.
- **`DungeonRun`** (`scripts/overworld/dungeon_run.gd`, a `RefCounted`) is the current expedition: the marker's data, `stages: Array[StageData]`, `index`, and a snapshot of the journey's progress at entry. `GameSession.dungeon` is null outside one.
- **`GameSession.enter_dungeon(marker)`:** saves the journey, builds the `DungeonRun`, sets `character`, `current_stage = stages[0]`, `run_progress = journey.progress`, zeroes the per-run counters (`run_kills`, `run_gold`) and loads `RUN`. The old `path` stays null in a dungeon run: `current_column` is not used (the end screen reads the dungeon's own index, below).
- **Stages (`DungeonRules`, section 10):** a cave has 1 stage, a boss `dungeon` 3, the `final_dungeon` 3; the stage's `theme` is `MapRegistry` for the marker's `map_id`; the layout seed is **fixed per marker and stage**: `GameSession.map_seed()` returns `hash("%s|%d" % [marker name, stage index])` inside a dungeon run (and keeps its old formula otherwise), so a dungeon always plays the same, and each of its stages a different one.
- **A stage is won** (`main.gd`'s existing win path): `GameSession.complete_stage()` in a dungeon run captures the progress into the journey and saves it; if more stages remain it advances `current_stage` and reloads `RUN` after the existing "Stage cleared" banner; on the last it shows the existing VICTORY end screen with its button changed to "Return to the world".
- **Cleared:** finishing the last stage adds the marker's name to `journey.cleared`, banks the run's gold (`bank_run()`), saves, and the button returns to the overworld standing just below the marker (so F is needed to re-enter). A cleared cave or dungeon can be entered again and replayed for XP and gold; it stays cleared.
- **Death:** `EndScreen` DEATH in a dungeon run reads "Return to the entrance"; the button calls `GameSession.leave_dungeon()`: the gold of the run is kept (as today, banked on death), the progress is the snapshot of the **last cleared stage** (XP and level-ups earned in the stage that was lost are lost; nothing already saved is lost), the marker is not cleared, and the player returns to the overworld at the marker.
- **Abandon:** the pause menu's Return to Hub in a dungeon run reads "Leave the dungeon"; its prompt "Leave the dungeon?" Yes calls the same `leave_dungeon()` (not the hub).
- **`EndScreen`** reads the dungeon's stage count for "Stages cleared n of N" (today it prints "of 3" fixed) and for "Stage reached"; its two buttons' labels follow the context. The old path-based run (select screen) is unchanged and still reachable from tests.
- **Quitting during a dungeon run** (Exit in the pause menu) leaves the journey as it last saved: the progress of the last cleared stage and the position at the marker; the stage in progress and the run's gold are lost (as an Exit from a stage is today).

## 9. Fog of war, the minimap and the world map

- **`FogMap`** (`scripts/overworld/fog_map.gd`, a `RefCounted`): a grid of 250 x 250 cells (8 x 8 tiles each) of revealed bytes. `reveal(world_pos, radius_tiles := 30)` marks the cells within the radius (a disc) and returns whether any changed; the overworld reveals every 0.25 s around the player and on loading. It serialises as a base64 string of 62,500 bytes in the journey. `ImageTexture` of the fog (L8 to an RGBA image: black with alpha 255 where unrevealed) is rebuilt only when a reveal changed something.
- **Minimap** (`scripts/ui/minimap.gd`, a `Control` in the top left of the overworld UI under the mana bar): 96 x 96 px, showing a 192-tile square (the region of `world_map.png` around the player, drawn from an `AtlasTexture`, 2 tiles per pixel) with the fog texture's matching region drawn over it, the player as a white dot, and the markers as small coloured dots (checkpoints yellow, caves and dungeons red, gates blue, specials green) only on revealed cells. A thin frame and, under it, the zone name of the player's tile (from `zones.png`, display names from the JSON).
- **World map** (`scripts/ui/world_map.gd`, a `Control`, opened by M, closed by M or Esc, pauses the tree): `world_map.png` scaled to fit the 640 x 360 view (about 0.17), nearest filter, the fog over it, the markers on revealed cells, the player's blinking dot, and the names of activated checkpoints. View only; no fast travel.
- The art file is the guide's map (the in-game world map, as it says). Rendering at that scale uses `texture_filter` linear for the downscale only on this screen.

## 10. Tuning as data: `DungeonRules` (`content/overworld/dungeon_rules.tres`)

`DungeonRules` (`scripts/overworld/dungeon_rules.gd`, a `Resource`):

| Field | Cave | Dungeon | Final vault |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stages` | 1 | 3 | 3 |
| `tier_offset` (added to the zone's tier) | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| `population_bonus` | 0 | 10 | 20 |
| `stage_step` (population and difficulty added per later stage) | 8 | 8 | 10 |

and `max_population` 60 (a cap on the final value: the old path's hardest stage is about +20, the alive cap is 300, and `crowd_bone_warren` already fails its frame-time limit, so nothing the rules build goes far above what has been played), `zone_tiers` (zones 0 and 1 easy, 2 to 5 normal, 6 and 7 hard), `population_per_zone` 4, `difficulty_per_zone` 3, `experience_per_zone` 3, `currency_per_zone` 2. `DungeonRules.stages_for(marker, rules) -> Array[StageData]` builds the stages: tier is `zone_tiers[zone] + tier_offset` clamped to hard; each modifier starts from the midpoint of `PathGenerator.RANGES` for that tier, plus the per-zone value times the zone index, plus the kind's bonus, plus `stage_step` times the stage index (population and difficulty only); `name` is the marker's name with " - stage n" for dungeons. These are first-pass numbers; the uncapped final-vault third stage would be about +83% (15 + 4 x 7 + 20 + 2 x 10), hence the cap of 60. A dungeon is 3 x 300 s = 15 minutes, shorter than the guide's 20 to 40: intentional for now (the user chose three stages). They are data and meant to be tuned in play. `OverworldRules` (`scripts/overworld/overworld_rules.gd`, static) holds the constants that are structure rather than tuning: `CHUNK_TILES` 32, the load radius, `SAFE_RADIUS_TILES` 14, the interaction radii and `gate_locked`.

## 15. Open questions / assumptions

- **O1 (assumed).** The overworld resumes at the **saved position**, not the last checkpoint, when you walk through the hub door; the respawn checkpoint is only for deaths (a later spec) and bad saves.
- **O2 (assumed).** Dying in a dungeon keeps the progress of the last cleared stage and loses the XP of the stage that was lost (the user said "everything is kept"; the XP of an unfinished stage is not yet part of the journey).
- **O3 (assumed: first-pass numbers).** The dungeon modifiers and the cave, dungeon and final-vault stage counts (1, 3, 3) are guesses against the old path's ranges. The final vault is three stages for now (the guide wants it biggest; it waits for the boss spec).
- **O4 (open, to be decided at the build by looking).** Whether the main road is drawn as a lighter ground tile along the `main_path` polyline. Without it the corridors read as plain ground; with it the route is easy to follow. Cheap to add once the tiles are seen.
- **O5 (risk, decided at step 2).** The eight tilesets are floor-tile sheets, not autotile sets. The catalog may end with flat fallback colours for some roles or zones; each gap is listed in the build notes and the user can supply tiles later.
- **O6 (assumed).** The guide says real structures are drawn at 16 px per tile; none exist, so all markers are code-drawn placeholders (as the hub's camp is).
- **O7 (assumed).** A cave or dungeon is entered by F and a prompt, never by walking into it, so returning to the overworld cannot re-trigger it.
- **O8 (assumed).** The old stage select and path generator stay in the code and their tests, with no door leading to them; removing them is a later cleanup.
- **O9 (existing).** `tests/maps/crowd_bone_warren.gd` fails its frame-time limit on `main`; the overworld adds no enemies, so it does not change that.
- **O10 (assumed).** Memory: the three 4 MB masks, the 1 MB fog and the 2000 x 2000 art texture (about 16 MB of video memory) are acceptable; the art could be split later if needed.
- **O11 (assumed).** The overworld HUD shows only the mana bar and the ability icons until enemies exist; hit points, XP and relic slots return with them.
- **O12 (assumed).** Per-journey `playtime` is stored for later use (a hub display); nothing reads it yet.

- **O13 (open).** Props (trees, rocks, bones, mushrooms) are not scattered in this spec: the ground and walls carry the biome look and the markers stand alone. The guide suggests scattering by biome with 14-tile clear zones around markers; it is a natural next step (or an addition here if wanted) and would reuse the seeded, per-chunk approach.
- **O14 (assumed).** The world is one fixed, hand-authored map shared by every journey; nothing about it is generated or saved per game (only the fog and the journey's state are). A random world per new game would be a different design.
- **O15 (note).** `JOY_BUTTON_BACK` is `hub_info` in the hub; the overworld's `world_map` action on the same button is a different scene, so they do not clash unless the two ever share input handling.

## 16. Definition of Done

- The hub door loads the overworld with the chosen character's own journey (new at the start camp, or resumed); three characters have three separate saves; gold, camp attributes and items are shared.
- The world matches the map tile for tile: the player cannot walk on a blocked tile anywhere, the corridors, trails, clearings and the seven 12-tile gates are as in `collision_v3.png`, biomes blend as `zones_v3.png` shows, blockers look solid with a visible edge; only the chunks near the player exist and the walk along the route stays smooth.
- All 108 markers stand at their world positions; checkpoints and the start camp save and set the respawn; gates are open (with a tested lock hook).
- A cave is one stage and a dungeon three, with fixed seeds, on the zone's map; clearing is saved and re-enterable; death and abandon return to the entrance keeping saved progress; a win returns below the marker with gold banked and progress saved.
- Fog, minimap and the world map work and are saved.
- All new tests pass; the whole suite passes except the known `crowd_bone_warren` failure; `.uid` and `.import` files are committed; READMEs are current; the open questions are answered or logged; nothing is claimed as tried by hand that was not.

## 17. Build notes

Built in three parallel parts (terrain, dungeon flow, fog and maps) on top of the world data and journey, then merged without conflicts; the scene, the markers and the hub door were built after the merge.

- **Terrain catalog:** all eight sheets share one layout, so every zone uses the same atlas cells for `ground` ((0,8) to (2,10)) and `blocker` ((1,1)); `blocker_deep`, `edge` and `shadow` have no tile in any sheet and use flat fallback colours (spec O5). Zone blends are hard-edged blotches that follow `zones.png`; the dither band of the spec is not built (the per-zone blend-pair colours are not in `ZoneTerrain`). The ground looks busy because the rounded floor tiles show dark gaps.
- **Gates:** the guide's `(nx, ny)` vector runs across the gap; the gap measures 10 to 21 tiles along it (the test accepts that range).
- **`FogMap`** keeps its own array and the overworld copies it into the journey before each save (`PackedByteArray` is a value type); `FogMap.image()` exists for tests because a texture reads back blank headless. The minimap's square is clamped near the map edges (the player dot moves off-centre there).
- **`PauseMenu.overworld_mode`** emits `return_to_hub_requested` and `exit_requested` so the overworld saves first; `WorldMap` opens itself on `world_map` and the overworld hides its prompt through the map's `visibility_changed` (the map pauses the tree).
- **`enter_dungeon`** loads the journey itself when none is loaded. A death or abandon also moves the journey position below the marker.
- `tests/overworld/overworld_walk.gd`: each 128 px path segment is walked in whole 60 px steps, so the walk is about 2,400 steps (the check allows 1,500 to 2,700).
- Not verified by hand: a real play-through (walking out of the hub door, resting, entering a cave and a dungeon and coming back), how the markers and the terrain look over a long walk, the memory and frame time of the 2000 x 2000 map texture on a real machine, and the minimap and world map at real window sizes. Props are not scattered (O13).
